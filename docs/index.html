Light, mid or heavy: which fabric weight suits you?

Fabric weight is given in gsm (grams per square metre). It is the quickest way to compare plain tees from different brands.

  • Light (approx. 140 to 150 gsm): airy and soft, good for summer, sport and layering.
  • Mid-weight (approx. 160 to 180 gsm): the everyday all-rounder for workwear, events and printing.
  • Heavy cotton (approx. 190 gsm and up): sturdy, less see-through and with a boxier drape.

As a rough guide, Fruit of the Loom's Original is approx. 145 gsm, Valueweight approx. 165 gsm and Heavy Cotton approx. 195 gsm. More in the T-shirt gsm guide. Buying for a team, a club or an event? See plain T-shirts in bulk.

What makes a good plain T-shirt?
Look for cotton, a fabric weight of approx. 150 to 190 gsm, a neckline that lies flat and a fit that suits you. Heavier fabric is less see-through and holds its shape longer. What does gsm mean on a T-shirt?
Gsm means grams per square metre and describes how heavy the fabric is. Light tees are approx. 140 to 150 gsm, mid-weight approx. 160 to 180 and heavy cotton tees approx. 190 gsm and up. Heavier fabric feels sturdier and is less see-through. Read the T-shirt gsm guide for details.
Do plain cotton T-shirts shrink?
Cotton can shrink a few percent, mostly on the first hot wash. Wash at 30 to 40 degrees, avoid a hot tumble dryer and choose your normal size rather than sizing down. If you are between two sizes, go for the larger one. That way the tee still fits after the first few washes.
